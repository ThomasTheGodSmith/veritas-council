How it works

Market Council helps a first-time investor stress-test a planned purchase before risking money.

Who it's for

A teenager with a few hundred dollars who wants to buy the Dow, or one company, and has set a limit on how much they're willing to lose.

What happens when you ask

  1. Eleven analysts, each a different kind of model we trained from scratch, predict what happens next: trend models, a gradient-boosted forecaster, a GARCH risk model, a text model of world news, a small transformer that writes possible futures, a CNN that reads the chart, a market-mood model and more.
  2. They debate. Where two disagree, the council checks who was right when they disagreed like this before. A Devil's Advocate estimates how often the majority is wrong in situations like today's.
  3. Your rules are checked in code: budget, maximum loss, holding period and sectors to avoid. Nothing can overrule a failed rule.
  4. The Judge decides how much to buy and where to put the stop-loss. The stop sits a little inside your limit, leaving room for a bad overnight jump in that stock.
  5. You approve or decline. Approving places a paper order. Declining with a reason sends your plan back to the council.

How we kept it honest

  • Models learned only from 1992–2019. The years 2020–2026 were kept in a separate folder the training code can't open, and automated tests check this.
  • Every setting was chosen on training years only, using robust rules that resist getting lucky.
  • We compare against simple strategies and report where the council loses, including on profit.
